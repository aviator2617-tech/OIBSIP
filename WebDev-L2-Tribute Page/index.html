A Life of Innovation

Grace Hopper was a computer scientist and United States Navy officer whose work helped shape modern computer programming.

Her Story and Legacy

Grace Brewster Murray Hopper was born in 1906 in New York City. She studied mathematics and physics and developed a lifelong interest in understanding how things worked.

During World War II, Hopper joined the United States Navy. She worked with the Harvard Mark I, an early electromechanical computer, and became involved in the emerging field of computing.

Hopper helped advance the idea that computers could be programmed using instructions closer to human language. Her work on early compilers helped lay important groundwork for making programming more accessible.

She also contributed to the development and promotion of COBOL, a programming language designed for business data processing. Her career continues to inspire people to explore, question, and innovate in computer science.
